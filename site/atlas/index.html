01

QualiaDB

Working examples in the lab

QualiaDB stores information with its relationships and supplies the query, reasoning and scientific libraries used across Webizen. A document, a source measurement and a permission can be examined together without treating them as interchangeable facts.

Its particular role

A result can stay connected to the source, circumstances and permissions that give it meaning. The same foundation supports storing those relationships, asking questions about them and applying an explicit rule before an action.

Why it matters

People need to understand why a result appeared, question its sources and correct mistakes. Those same capabilities help a developer trace a fault and help an organisation explain a decision.

A use to picture

A council investigating creek restoration could compare a terrain source with its description and provenance before using it in planning. The current public terrain dataset provides a small, inspectable starting point; it is not a restoration assessment.

People, rights and agency

Context, provenance and permission checks support informed use of information. They are tools for implementing safeguards, not a blanket guarantee that every application meets human rights obligations.

02

Webizen applications and runtime

Working examples in the lab

Webizen is the human-facing application environment built around QualiaDB. Desktop, Studio and application interfaces bring knowledge, creative work and authorised actions into places where a person can use them.

Its particular role

Looking at a tool, adding its resources and allowing it to act are separate choices. That distinction gives a person a chance to understand an application before giving it access to information or capabilities.

Why it matters

A useful computing environment should make the next action understandable and let people work towards their own purposes. It should make room for assistance, review and changing circumstances.

A use to picture

A person preparing a community presentation can choose a public dataset, check where it came from and keep the actual query result with their notes.

People, rights and agency

Application controls must preserve the difference between a person's choices and another party's authority. An accessible explanation and a visible result are part of meaningful control, not merely visual decoration.

Webizen Host Aether Poet

Current demonstration scope

The new native website host supplies dataset, query and selected proof instruments. It is not a qualification of all Webizen Desktop, Studio or QApp workflows.

03

Poet

Demonstration still to come

Poet is the authoring application associated with projects, documents and creative work in the ecosystem. Its role is to help people shape a work, while shared libraries supply the underlying language, knowledge and production services.

Its particular role

Poet is the place for shaping and revising a work; Synth is the producer that turns its recipes into deliverable resources. Keeping editing and production distinct lets a creator revisit the work without losing the account of how an earlier version was made.

Why it matters

Creators should be able to revise a work while keeping its sources and meaning available. A project should remain understandable to its collaborators and future maintainers.

A use to picture

A planned exhibit will follow a creator changing one scene while keeping its sources, attribution and earlier version available to collaborators.

People, rights and agency

Authorship, attribution and permission to use a source need to survive editing and export. The website must show these relationships in a real workflow before claiming the authoring experience complete.

04

VibeScript

Working examples in the lab

VibeScript expresses a piece of work as checked source: calculations, authored resources and the capabilities a host is asked to provide. The language engine lives in QualiaDB; the separate vibe-script repository supplies editing and language tools.

Its particular role

The instructions for a work are kept as source that can be checked and run again. They name what the host is asked to do, so a change to the work can be reviewed rather than disappearing into a sequence of clicks.

Why it matters

A person can revisit how a result was produced, and a developer can reproduce or repair it. That helps creative work remain editable and makes technical explanations more concrete.

A use to picture

Using a native production recipe, a creator can change a source wavelength and compare the resulting data while keeping the units and model assumptions visible. This is a production workflow, not a live wavelength-editing control in the current website.

People, rights and agency

Naming a capability does not itself grant authority to use personal information or a device. Host admission and a person's permission remain separate responsibilities.

Works alongside
Synth Etalon QualiaDB

Current demonstration scope

Native Vibe recipes produce this proof's assets and control the world. The editor, language-service and complete family catalogue have not all been recreated as website instruments.

05

Synth

Working examples in the lab

Synth turns VibeScript recipes and retained inputs into resources such as geometry, images and field data. It packages the source and outputs so that a work can be reopened and checked.

Its particular role

Synth keeps the instructions and retained inputs with the produced resources, and checks that recorded production can be replayed. A creator or reviewer can trace an output back to the work that made it.

Why it matters

An impressive picture is more useful when its underlying work is inspectable and reproducible. Creators can continue editing; reviewers can distinguish a generated illustration from an acquired measurement.

A use to picture

A filmmaker or educator can revise a scene, retain its inputs and compare the new output with an earlier version. The current proof exposes that production trail for its authored field and assets.

People, rights and agency

Keeping source and provenance with a work helps preserve attribution and permitted use. A successful replay proves reproducibility of the recorded production, not ownership of every possible input.

Hypermedia production VibeScript Etalon

06

Hypermedia production

Working examples in the lab

Hypermedia production supplies composable authoring libraries for geometry, surfaces, images, typography, layout, motion, sound and other media. Synth and other applications use these libraries to build editable works.

Its particular role

The shared libraries let a model, its materials, movement, sound and written explanation be authored as related parts of a work. Their transformations can retain inspectable sources instead of reducing the whole work to a finished picture.

Why it matters

The same underlying work can support an illustration, an accessible explanation and an inspectable technical instrument. It need not become a disconnected promotional image each time it changes form.

A use to picture

An educator could use the same authored subject for a spatial view, a written explanation and an interactive lesson, keeping clear which parts are illustrations and which come from evidence.

People, rights and agency

A composition must retain source terms and the meaning of its data across transformations. Availability of an operation is separate from permission to publish its inputs or from artistic acceptance of its output.

07

Etalon

Demonstration still to come

Etalon helps Webizen use graphics and specialist processors to calculate, handle signals and present results. Its direction includes light and other electromagnetic fields; visual rendering is one use of those foundations.

Its particular role

A calculation or sensor reading needs both a dependable execution path and an account of what the result means. Etalon supplies execution building blocks; QualiaDB connects their results to context, permissions and evidence.

Why it matters

People should be able to see what was computed and what an observation can support. Device limits and uncertain measurements matter when a visualisation is used to understand the physical world.

A use to picture

An educator examining how light is represented can compare the authored field values with their visual display. The present instrument shows a modelled snapshot, not a reading from a sensor.

People, rights and agency

Access to a sensor or accelerator does not confer permission to observe a person. Sensing needs a declared purpose and appropriate authority; technical output must not be mistaken for consent.

Works alongside
Anvil QualiaDB Synth

Current demonstration scope

The proof uses the ecosystem's native field and rendering path. It does not establish a general Etalon waveform generator, RF transmitter or complete sensing/inference demonstration.

08

Anvil

Demonstration still to come

Anvil describes what a device can actually do, so an application can choose work that suits the available hardware and explain its limits. It supplies hardware facts used by other parts of the ecosystem.

Its particular role

It separates what a device reports from what a person permits. The hardware report can guide an execution choice without being treated as authority to access a vault or as a description of the person using the device.

Why it matters

Software should respond honestly to the computer someone has. Reporting a limit or choosing a qualified path is more useful than displaying a control that silently does something else.

A use to picture

A planned demonstration will show why the same creative task takes a different execution path on a laptop and a more capable workstation, using actual device reports and measurements.

People, rights and agency

A device identifier is not a person. Hardware observations can inform a policy decision, but must not replace a person's agency or become an undeclared tracking mechanism.

Etalon Aether Webizen Host

Aether

Demonstration still to come

Aether is the application framework being developed for native Webizen interfaces. It brings components, state changes and rendering together so that applications can respond to the person using them.

Its particular role

Its native application direction brings interface components, rendering and VibeScript together through shared ecosystem interfaces. The aim is for changes to a work and the controls a person uses to stay connected across presentations.

Why it matters

People need interfaces that explain changes, remain usable under real device constraints and offer more than one way to reach a task. That work belongs in the application framework as well as individual pages.

A use to picture

A future exhibit will exercise the same task through keyboard, readable controls and a spatial presentation, with observable state changes.

People, rights and agency

Supporting different needs requires tested navigation, understandable state and accessible alternatives. A framework's architectural intention is not proof that every application already provides them.

Chora

Demonstration still to come

Chora is an embeddable web and spatial document engine. It gives a host application a way to render traditional web content and documents while exposing explicit interfaces to the surrounding application.

Its particular role

A document can be rendered inside an application while its permissions and security-sensitive actions remain under the host’s control. That boundary matters when ordinary web content appears beside a person’s local tools or records.

Why it matters

A human centric ecosystem still needs to read and use the existing web. Clear boundaries help people move between documents and applications without making every embedded page equally trusted.

A use to picture

A planned exhibit will compare an ordinary embedded document with an explicitly authorised host action.

People, rights and agency

Origin isolation and delegated permissions are practical places to enforce boundaries. The website must demonstrate actual refusal and authorised access before claiming a particular guardianship or privacy outcome.

Webizen Host

Demonstration still to come

Webizen Host is being developed to run applications and shared services on Linux servers, with records of who maintains them, what resources they use and which responsibilities are shared.

Its particular role

Its design separates publishing a name, running an application, limiting its resources and deciding who may act for a group. An account name or domain is treated as a technical handle, not as the entirety of a person.

Why it matters

People and communities need understandable choices about where a service runs, who maintains it and how shared costs and responsibilities are handled.

A use to picture

A future instrument will inspect a bounded deployment's namespace, resource limits and stewardship record before an authorised action.

People, rights and agency

A hosting account must not be treated as the whole person. Isolation, delegated authority and accountability need end-to-end evidence, especially when a service acts for a family or cooperative.

Web Civics and semantic instruments

Demonstration still to come

Web Civics supplies shared terms for describing human agency, agreements and normative instruments: documents stating rights, duties or agreed rules. People and software can trace those terms to definitions and sources, and validate how an application uses them.

Its particular role

Its terms are organised around people, shared agreements and the sources of normative instruments. Keeping an instrument’s wording and provenance available helps someone check what a rule actually refers to before relying on it.

Why it matters

A shared term is useful only if people can discover what it means and where it came from. Clear definitions help communities, developers and institutions discuss the same subject without silently changing it.

A use to picture

A planned instrument will let a community group inspect the terms used in an agreement and distinguish the source text from the particular rule an application evaluates. The present proof exercises only selected authored rule fixtures.

People, rights and agency

Encoding an instrument is not the same as resolving a legal question or enforcing a right. Preserve its source and scope, and show exactly which authored rule an evaluator applied.

Wellfare, cooperation and the work graph

Demonstration still to come

These QualiaDB libraries describe welfare support, shared projects, contributions, records and delegated assistance. They provide structures for cooperation without reducing a person to their account or a single task.

Its particular role

A correction can preserve the earlier record rather than erase it. Assistance can be limited to a purpose, time and authority, with consent and revocation kept explicit; permission to help with one task is not permission to take over someone’s life.

Why it matters

Care and shared work involve changing needs and relationships. Keeping who may do what, for whom and under which conditions visible helps people cooperate without losing their own voice.

A use to picture

A planned public scenario will delegate one limited task, revoke it and show the actual evaluator refusing the later action.

People, rights and agency

Support should preserve agency rather than assume unlimited substitute authority. Real consent, revocation and consequential-decision evidence need to be exercised in the eventual native workflow.

Its particular role

The modelling requirements keep named structures, their sources and their relationships available for inspection. That makes the accuracy of a particular part something a reviewer can examine, rather than judge only from the appearance of a whole body.

Why it matters

Spatial explanations can help people understand a body and discuss support needs. Their usefulness depends on recognisable structure and clear limits, not just on producing a renderable file.

A use to picture

A future native anatomy exhibit will let a person select a named structure and inspect both its geometry and modelling evidence.

People, rights and agency

Educational models should support comprehension and dignity. They must not imply a diagnosis, a personal body measurement or clinical validation that the source does not establish.

Ecopunk and living-world work

Demonstration still to come

Ecopunk connects environmental source registers, biological knowledge and authored living-world experiences. Its current sources include Australian, Earth-space and socioeconomic discovery registers and procedural botanical studies.

Its particular role

It keeps a discovered source separate from material that has been reviewed for publication, retaining licence, sensitivity and provenance along the way. An artist’s tree remains an illustration rather than becoming an unsupported claim that the species was observed there.

Why it matters

Understanding a place means relating living things, physical conditions and human activity. Keeping those relationships inspectable helps an attractive landscape become a place to learn and investigate.

A use to picture

Compare the public elevation data for Canberra with the separately labelled authored woodland and inspect each source’s provenance.

People, rights and agency

Sensitive ecological and community knowledge needs appropriate authority and careful disclosure. A public scene should not expose private travel or imply that a species was observed where an artist placed it.

16

QDNF and the identifier fabric

Demonstration still to come

QDNF supplies native protocol libraries for exchanging information between devices: establishing sessions, routing messages and recognising the authority attached to an exchange. Related identifier policy remains within QualiaDB.

Its particular role

The rules for an exchange are kept separate from the particular connection carrying it. That allows authority and disclosure conditions to be examined alongside a message path, rather than assuming that being connected is enough.

Why it matters

People need to know where information can travel and what authority accompanies an exchange. Naming, transport and permission should be inspectable instead of being collapsed into a single login.

A use to picture

A planned exhibit will follow a limited exchange between devices and show a real refusal when the requested action falls outside the stated authority.

People, rights and agency

A cryptographic handle identifies a particular technical relationship, not the entirety of a person. Human agency and disclosure limits must survive changes of device, network and institution.
