# Webizen: complete native website and experience overhaul

**Planning baseline: 16 November 2026, Australia/Sydney.**

**Status: implementation plan, not a claim of completed artwork or migrated demonstrations.** This replaces the earlier plan's acceptance criteria wherever they allowed a source link, a working renderer or a catalogue entry to stand in for the actual experience. The existing native preview is an engineering prototype with a failed visual review. It is not the quality benchmark for this programme.

## 1. Outcome and non-negotiable requirements

Build an extraordinary, coherent, explorable **human centric computing ecosystem**. Space, Earth, life, human society and the infosphere are different scales of the same experience. The website must make the ecosystem understandable to ordinary people, researchers, government and UN-related audiences, developers, AI clients and investors. It must show what each component does, what distinguishes its approach, why it matters to people, and what evidence supports the claim.

The work includes the entire website: entry experience, environments, navigation, module explanations, underlying datasets, working demonstrations, tests, benchmarks, documentation, accessible reading and operation, search, internationalisation, evidence, downloads and publication. A better campsite alone does not fulfil this brief.

Mandatory constraints:

1. **The data and demonstrations belong inside the new experience.** Every migrated demonstration has its actual inputs, execution, controls, outputs and inspectable evidence there. An old-page link, iframe, screenshot, recorded result or decorative scene does not count as an implemented demonstration.
2. **Native platform throughout the world.** QualiaDB, VibeScript, Synth, Hypermedia production and Etalon perform the work. Browser JavaScript is limited to generated WASM interoperability/loading. No authored JavaScript scene, camera, simulation, interaction, data-query implementation or replacement scientific algorithm. Preserved legacy code can serve as a development reference or comparison oracle outside the production application.
3. **Real work, honestly identified.** Use the actual engine, codec, solver, model or producer. Distinguish measured observations, authored fixtures, simulations, derived values, illustrative artwork and historical results. Never fabricate benchmark values, competitors, scientific accuracy or native provenance.
4. **Art is a release requirement.** Successful compilation, HTTP responses, triangle counts, populated graphs and passing assertions do not certify visual quality. Asset and assembled-scene reviews are separate requirements.
5. **Complete coverage, not selective highlights.** Reconcile every old site entry, underlying dependency and current core-repository capability. Every required capability must be integrated, consolidated as a proven equivalent without loss, or excluded only by an explicit user-approved scope change recorded in the ledger. Group equivalent presentations without losing their data, behaviours or meaning. Record remaining gaps explicitly.
6. **Human rights, purposes, needs and support requirements are operational.** Demonstrate consent, refusal, purpose restrictions, provenance, correction, accessible participation and explainable decisions through actual implemented mechanisms. Do not imply that one deontic demonstration proves universal legal compliance.
7. **Australian English is the default.** `en-AU` is a valid language tag and must remain the default regardless of browser preference. The architecture must accommodate all languages, scripts, directions and locale-specific formats. Translation coverage is reported honestly.
8. **Retain personal context with dignity.** The campsite remains the user's lived creative context: gold 2012/13 Passat wagon, streamlined charcoal/black roof box, wind-up Jayco camper with canvas and slide-out beds, solar arrays of 3 + 3 + 2 panels, and a Starlink Mini. Journey data is limited to the approved broad regions/month bands. Do not publish exact campsite coordinates or raw Timeline data.
9. **Extensible and reproducible.** New realms, modules, datasets, instruments, languages and presentations register through versioned contracts. Native production inputs, dependencies, output identities and review evidence travel with the work.

## 2. Why the present work must be overhauled

The native migration retained important geometry but dropped essential surface and environmental information. The audit found four flat terrain material bands instead of continuous surface variation; missing authored UVs and the existing Synth ground normal map; six near and twelve distant trees in place of a much denser layered forest; missing riverbank boulders; inadequate environment lighting; and weak framing at several destinations. The native version passed functional checks while failing the visual brief.

The earlier Three.js version is a **minimum regression reference**, not the final artistic target. Its generic trees, sparse ground cover and limited demonstrations also fall short. Recovery must restore its stronger qualities, then exceed them using the actual native platform. There is no source-backed conclusion here that Hypermedia lacks the necessary production capabilities.

Current reusable work includes native asset recipes and geometry, approved campsite details, real Q42 publication, native WASM integration, some real test and benchmark adapters, dataset/result baselines and source inventories. Reuse is conditional on an individual evidence review. Existing "accepted" flags do not automatically carry into this programme.

## 4. One world across scales

Use a consistent spatial, typographic, material, sonic and narrative language. Scale changes should be understandable: the visitor can move from a beam of light to a leaf, a catchment, a community and an orbital view while retaining the selected subject, question and evidence. Each realm has a memorable scene, a useful human question and functioning instruments. Visitors can also use a direct instrument index or reading interface without walking through the world.

| Realm | Human invitation and composition | Working demonstrations and underlying data |
|---|---|---|
| **The field studio** | A carefully observed Australian campsite: lived work, care, energy and connection. Intimate objects, credible ground contact, vegetation, weathered materials and an open horizon. | Energy/system studies where actual solvers exist; native media production; local knowledge and authorship; inspectable asset lineage; the approved journey chapters. The camp connects to the ecosystem, rather than occupying the whole experience. |
| **Earth and the living catchment** | Travel between an orbital globe, regional terrain, riverbank, soil and canopy. Water and landscape make spatial scale and uncertainty tangible. | Geodesy, coordinates, GIS/raster/point-cloud data, terrain, hydrology, soil, climate/fire studies, map layers, spatial queries, native `.m64`/`.10d` inspection. Source-defined resolution and datum remain visible. |
| **The living library** | A botanical garden and biological observatory with recognisable species and meaningful transitions from organism to organ and cellular scale. | Taxonomy, occurrences, botanical traits, species corpus, anatomy, ontology picking, biomechanics and scientific models. Distinguish actual specimens and measurements from comparative scaffolds and authored illustrations. |
| **Space and the observatory** | A night observatory that opens into the Earth–Moon system and larger astronomical frames. Deliberate darkness, scale cues, trajectories and readable instruments. | Existing cosmic-coordinate/OCS, orbital, astronomical, solar/geomagnetic and scientific capabilities; actual datasets or labelled fixtures; units, epochs, numerical settings and error checks. Camera zoom alone is not a multiscale-coordinate demonstration. |
| **Light, fields and sound** | An optical/acoustic workshop spanning sunlight, material, RF/EM fields and spatial listening. | **The commons** | A human-scale civic space: shared resources, agreements, work, care and competing needs. | **The infosphere** | A navigable knowledge environment tied to subjects encountered elsewhere: sources, assertions, conflicts, time, identity and relationships acquire spatial expression. | Actual graph data, queries, ontology/SHACL/reasoning, identifiers/QDNF, cells/leases/streaming where implemented, source inspection, version changes and uncertainty. Outputs remain downloadable and reopenable. |
| **The proof laboratory** | A calm, precise environment where the visitor can test the claims encountered anywhere else. Instruments should express measurements clearly rather than decorating a dashboard with a 3D room. | All migrated assertions, benchmark workloads, raw samples, historical records, engine profiles, devices, solver checks and failure cases. Show real requests, results and constrained failures; do not simulate a working service with an animation. |

These are connected environments and presentation modes over shared data. They are not ten disconnected microsites. A source dataset, entity, experiment and result keep the same identity in the world, accessible interface, API and downloaded evidence.

## 5. Complete data and demonstration migration

### 5.1 Reconcile the old site before building substitutes

The fresh planning audit reports **204 current HTML routes**: 81 source pages, 63 generated anatomy previews, 39 historical documents, 11 release pages, 8 benchmark entries, one template and one test entry. These are not 204 distinct computational demonstrations. Preserve the distinction and inspect each route's scripts, workers, fixtures, resources, engine bindings and interaction states.

The baseline additionally records **44 test-suite registrations** and **five historical benchmark-result JSON records**. Dataset descriptors and result records are separate inventories. Do not conflate these counts with unique tests, executed tests or workload completeness.

For each legacy feature, create a migration record containing:

- Stable feature ID; all originating routes and source revisions; owning repository/module/function.
- Human purpose and explanation; current behaviour; inputs and default values; every meaningful control and output state, including errors and cancellation.
- Underlying datasets and fixtures; local/remote resource identities; licences, attribution, units, CRS/epoch, sensitivity, provenance and version.
- Actual execution path and requirements: browser WASM, native service, GPU provider, model/device dependency or recorded historical artifact.
- New realm, instrument and accessible equivalent; data inspector and export route; source-code/evidence reference.
- Baseline oracle, regression fixtures, expected semantics, numerical tolerances and visual comparison requirements.
- Implementation status, owner, dependency, unresolved issue and acceptance evidence. **Initial state is unimplemented until independently verified.**

Historical publications and release notes become native searchable records and readable documents inside the new site. Generated anatomy previews become navigable source-qualified assets with equivalent inspection and context. Templates can be superseded by the new shell, but their unique information must not disappear. Redirects are navigation continuity only.

### 5.2 Data is part of the delivered product

Every instrument ships with a usable default dataset or explicitly declared acquisition path. The dataset browser offers search, filters, definitions, provenance, versions, schema, samples and complete export or bounded retrieval of the full permitted corpus. Large data is streamed by partition; it is not silently replaced by a tiny sample while retaining claims about the full dataset.

Use Q42 graphs for identities, assertions, relationships, semantic descriptions, rights and publication metadata. Retain dense media, rasters, meshes, signals, volumes and model weights in the appropriate existing native formats with graph references. Do not expand dense arrays into triples merely to claim that everything is graph-driven. Preserve original snapshots in the authoring/evidence area and publish authorised derivatives through a manifest.

Migration must cover data embedded in JavaScript, JSON, CSV, binary buffers, shader resources, example code and downloaded fixtures—not just files under `docs/data/`. Data fetched by old demos must be pinned or clearly declared live, with an honest offline/dependency state. Restricted data stays restricted; provide a separate, explicitly labelled public fixture for a public demonstration without claiming it is the protected source.

### 5.3 A demonstration completion contract

The visitor must be able to **understand → inspect inputs → change them → run the real operation → observe results → inspect evidence → export/reopen** without being sent to the old webpage.

Required instrument states: ready, loading data, running, progress where available, cancelling, succeeded, invalid input, unsupported capability, dependency unavailable and failed. Show actual results and useful errors. A disabled control or a prerecorded cinematic cannot be counted as a working capability.

Simple visits expose a small set of meaningful controls. Advanced views reveal full supported parameters, schemas, native output, logs and reproducibility details. Both views operate on the same run. Human prose precedes code and engine names; technical evidence remains directly accessible.

2. A concrete distinguishing mechanism supported by source and a demonstration, not an unsupported superiority claim.
3. At least one consequential role within a cross-module visitor journey.
4. Its complete mapped capability/instrument coverage, dependencies and limitations.
5. A developer path with native examples, interface/schema contracts and executable checks.
6. Version, owner, source and evidence records that can be inspected by people and AI clients.

The catalogue of 150 canonical Synth morphisms must be enumerated from current source and mapped individually. A small gallery representing twelve categories is insufficient. Conversely, the 150 morphisms do not exhaust QualiaDB, Etalon or the other repositories; use their current public interfaces, existing demonstrations and source registries to extend coverage.

## 7. Native architecture and repository decision

Keep **`webizen-website` as the dedicated website repository**. It owns the publication graph, world/exhibit registry, Vibe works, source acquisition manifests, authored assets, native web host, instrument UI, editorial content, locale catalogues, tests and release manifest. It does not fork the platform algorithms into a website-specific substitute engine.

Pin component revisions and build identities. Platform changes belong in their owning repositories with narrow contracts and tests. Use actual dependencies and existing codecs/providers. A missing website binding is an integration task; it must not be reported as an absent underlying capability without checking the function registry and source.

Proposed organisation:

```text
world/realms/              Vibe scene, interaction and presentation works
world/assets/             authored sources and asset manifests
instruments/              native UI and versioned execution contracts
data/catalogue/           dataset descriptors and acquisition receipts
data/publications/        Q42 and permitted dense-data packs
content/                  human-facing module/subject/editorial records
locales/                  en-AU source and reviewed translations
native/                   browser/native application host and adapters
packages/                 HMC/native asset bundles and dependency indices
evidence/                 immutable run, visual and migration evidence
tests/                    differential, integrity and user-flow checks
release/                  explicit public-file manifests and redirects
```

World state, subject selection, observer paths and instrument definitions come from Q42 and Vibe. Rust/WASM hosts browser interoperability and actual native execution/rendering. Server-side native services are legitimate for capabilities that require them, with real bounded requests and visible execution profiles. No silent browser imitation replaces a server/GPU/model capability.

Texture handling is a first-class asset contract: native MAT1 texture references, source digests, colour space, UV01, tangent frames, residency and actual shader use must agree. The known existing routes are attributed GLB material import **or** unassigned geometry plus explicit native material binding; mixing both incorrectly must fail. A neutral fallback texture may support an intentional loading state, but cannot pass final visual acceptance as the authored material.

Native `.emf` remains the durable electromagnetic field; `.10d` supports its references and mesh, `.m64` its applicable spatial data. Visual and acoustic conversions must identify their common source rather than inventing a second field. Use existing platform formats and interfaces for other modalities.

Public distribution uses reviewed packs, hashes and bounded streaming. Keep source caches, private inputs, executable build helpers and raw personal information outside the public manifest. Share the same release identity between the dedicated site and main versions published on webizen.au. Local review is not public deployment.

## 8. Asset production and artistic requirements

The [asset bible](ART-DIRECTION-AND-ASSET-BIBLE.md) specifies the work in depth. The production programme must create environments and assets, not merely arrange a few existing models on a flat surface.

Required asset families include georeferenced terrain/river/coast/soil; rock and ground-contact transitions; botanical canopy/understorey/grass/litter with appropriate variety and ecological placement; fauna and restrained environmental motion; the campsite and lived objects; observatory/space assets and scale references; anatomy and biological specimens; civic/common spaces; optical/acoustic instruments; studio machinery and authored media; knowledge/data visual forms; the proof laboratory; typography, icons, tactile interaction surfaces, soundscapes and transition cinematics.

For each hero asset and family record: source/reference, intended silhouette and scale, ecological/functional context, geometry recipe, material layers, UV/tangent policy, rig/animation, LOD/streaming variants, collision/picking, accessibility description, provenance, dependency hashes, review cameras and budget measurements. Variants should preserve identity and silhouette; destructive decimation that leaves floating fragments is rejected.

Production sequence: references and measured requirements → silhouette/blocking → native geometry → UV/surface/material → lighting/animation → native front/side/three-quarter review → assembled scene review → interactive performance qualification. Review material charts and asset turntables before judging an entire environment. Photograph-to-material methods need appropriate projection and attribution; a projected photo is not automatically a complete physically correct texture set.

Environmental composition must provide foreground, middle distance, background and readable destinations. Clearings are deliberately shaped, not the absence of production. Rivers require believable banks and transitions; trees require communities and understorey rather than isolated copies. Space scenes need scale, orientation and reference frames rather than a black background with glowing particles. Infosphere scenes need meaningful spatial encodings and legible results rather than generic connected nodes.

### Ecological and scientific sourcing

Reuse Ecopunk's Australian, Earth/space and socioeconomic registers. First acquisitions should establish a bounded public landscape independent of private travel records: actual terrain and bioregion/vegetation context, then source-qualified species occurrences and traits. Candidate sources include ELVIS/GA/NSW Spatial, DEA, Geofabric, IBRA/NVIS, ALA, NSL/APC/APNI, TERN, BioNet and AusTraits. The local register is a discovery source; verify the specific release, payload and attribution before ingestion.

Preserve taxon identifiers, trait definitions, units, uncertainty, dates and record-level licences. Do not treat an authored local flora catalogue as measured evidence. Retain published generalisation of sensitive locations. Artistic interpretations can enrich the environment, but must remain identifiable as such when inspected.

## 9. Rights, observer status and human significance

Visitor paths respond to declared purpose and support preferences, plus the actual authority context where an authenticated capability genuinely requires it. Do not infer sensitive identity, political alignment, disability or entitlement from behaviour. Explain and allow changes to presentation preferences; keep anonymous exploration useful.

The observer model distinguishes purpose, experience level, language, accessibility/support needs, selected subject, current question, device capability, consent and authenticated authority. These are not interchangeable. Choosing "researcher", "government" or "investor" changes explanation and order; it does not grant privileges or suppress inconvenient evidence.

Build scenario suites that demonstrate: a permitted aggregate request and refused individual request; withdrawal or expiry of permission; conflicting assertions and correction with lineage; purpose-limited reuse; an accessible equivalent interaction; attribution of authored work; and cooperation/resource decisions with inspectable assumptions. Use actual implemented evaluators, not a prewritten green/red label. Include negative cases and counterfactual input changes.

Connect information geometry (structure, provenance, transport), knowledge geometry (evidence, concepts, inference, uncertainty) and socioeconomic geometry (people, institutions, work, resources, obligations and effects). The same visible link must have a defined predicate and a way to inspect its supporting data. Layout is not evidence of causation.

Audience journeys are explicit acceptance fixtures: an ordinary visitor understands and completes a meaningful task; a developer runs and modifies a native example; a researcher inspects methods/data; a government/UN-related visitor explores institutions, rights and consequences; an investor inspects demonstrated capabilities, maturity and unresolved work; an AI client retrieves the same structured publication and evidence. No audience receives fabricated certainty or a separate set of facts.

Concrete task fixtures must include:

- **Ordinary visitor:** begin with a human question about sharing information or understanding a place; inspect a small intelligible dataset; change a permission or environmental parameter; run the actual operation; explain the changed outcome without needing to read engine code.
- **Government / UN-related visitor:** inspect a source-qualified regional environmental or socioeconomic question; see data scale, uncertainty, institutional context and permitted-use limits; compare actual outputs; export the evidence and assumptions. A diagram of an SDG or a rights principle is not the demonstration.
- **Researcher:** reopen a pinned run, inspect the complete permitted input and method, change a parameter and compare numerical outputs with uncertainty/tolerance information.
- **Developer:** inspect a working Vibe/native instrument, change its bounded input, obtain real exported output and a failing-input explanation, then follow the exact module/interface owner.
- **Investor:** trace an understandable user task through the contributing modules, inspect real maturity and execution evidence, and run a correctly scoped workload. Visual polish cannot be used to imply market, adoption or performance facts that have not been established.
- **AI client:** retrieve the same versioned entity/dataset/instrument manifests, query actual structured evidence and exercise explicitly permitted capabilities through the supported interface. Machine-readable access observes the same data and authority boundaries.

## 10. Tests, benchmarks and the proof laboratory

Preserve original assertions, fixtures, expected outcomes and benchmark records before changing presentation. Port browser-only orchestration and UI into the native instrument framework; use original harnesses as external differential references where appropriate. Do not reduce coverage to the subset easiest to run in WASM. Native, service, GPU, inference and device-dependent profiles retain their actual requirements and identities.

Every test family and benchmark workload receives an in-environment instrument, searchable listing, readable result, run/cancel controls, raw export and failure explanation. Aggregate status is generated from real run records, including skips and exclusions. Historical results remain historical and cannot be substituted for a current run.

Benchmark contracts record dataset/version/hash, engine/build, backend/device, workload semantics, preparation, warm-up, timed interval, samples, correctness checks, cancellation, errors, raw values and comparability. Separate preparation, transfer, query/compute, rendering and end-to-end timings. Stop scene animation and isolate competing work during comparative runs. Run one benchmark job per measured device.

The previously corrected canonical 60-bit synthetic hash behaviour must remain explicit, with a regression fixture. Do not combine corrected and historical incompatible series. A two-step point-query workload cannot be relabelled as a single SPARQL join. Show distributions and units, not only a flattering headline number. Publish competitor comparisons only from reproducible matching workloads and environments.

Source bugs receive a reproducer, owner, narrow fix, regression evidence and downstream validation. The 114 candidate source-reference issues from the old inventory need triage; they are neither 114 confirmed runtime defects nor a completed repair list.

## 11. Accessibility, internationalisation and presentation

The accessible interface is an equal implementation of the new instruments and data, not an escape link to an old page. All essential tasks work through keyboard and semantic controls, with focus management, readable structure, error identification and screen-reader output. Do not make essential text or controls exclusive to the 3D canvas. Provide motion, sound, contrast, text-size and low-resource choices without diminishing access to the underlying data.

Use `en-AU` source text, BCP 47 locale identities, explicit persistence, documented fallback and per-message language metadata. Support RTL and mixed-direction text, shaping, font coverage, line breaking, translated units/date/number conventions, expanded text and input methods. Exercise representative scripts and pseudolocalisation before accepting the foundation. "All languages supported" means an extensible technical foundation; do not claim all translations exist until they have been produced and reviewed.

Separate visiting from operating: a short invitation, a visible subject/action, progressive disclosure of controls, and an evidence view. Keep instructional prose human and concrete. Avoid engine names as the primary navigation and avoid copying internal implementation labels into every visitor-facing sentence.

## 12. Quality definition and acceptance gates

"AAA" is the requested quality target, not a certification or a claim conferred by this plan. Acceptance needs both demanding technical evidence and a convincing artistic result. The following gates apply to every deliverable, then to the complete release.

| Gate | Evidence required | Automatic failure examples |
|---|---|---|
| G0 — scope and sources | Complete old-route/data/capability mapping; pinned source owners; unknowns assigned. | Unmapped feature; invented API; treating a repository name as evidence of execution. |
| G1 — production integrity | Real native recipes/outputs, hashes, materials, provenance, decoded data and reproducible production where supported. | Missing texture silently replaced; fabricated provenance; output count offered as visual proof. |
| G2 — asset quality | Actual native front/side/three-quarter and material reviews; silhouette, proportion, detail and scale checks. | Placeholder anatomy described as a finished organ; broken LOD; floating parts; unreviewed asset. |
| G3 — assembled art | Matched-camera desktop/mobile captures, overlays on/off, lighting/material checks and independent scene critique. At least parity with the strongest previous views, then fulfilment of the new art direction. | Bare terrain masquerading as a completed biome; texture loss; unreadable materials; empty destinations; composition obscured by UI. |
| G4 — real instrument | Input changes cause appropriate actual runtime changes; run/cancel/errors/results/data inspection/export work inside the new experience. | Legacy link/iframe; static recording labelled live; substituted algorithm; missing underlying data. |
| G5 — evidence and rights | Dataset identity, provenance, units, authority and unsupported states visible; negative scenarios verified. | Unlabelled simulation; suppressed error; preference interpreted as permission; leaked precise/private data. |
| G6 — access and devices | Keyboard, assistive reading, representative locales, motion/audio controls, mobile, resize and recovery flows. | English silently becomes US English; RTL breaks controls; inaccessible canvas-only task; white/fake fallback presented as the full work. |
| G7 — performance | Recorded target device/build, loading/residency, p50/p95 frame and interaction times, memory and sustained runs. | Performance achieved by silently dropping authored geometry/materials or measurement work. |
| G8 — complete release | Every required coverage record has independently verified completion; redirects and all in-site paths resolve; release manifest reproducible and reviewed. | Claims of full overhaul while realms, datasets, native Lab instruments or module coverage remain deferred. |

For art review, independently score composition, form/detail, material/lighting, ecological/contextual coherence, motion/sound, interaction/readability and originality on a five-point rubric with written reasons and screenshots. A score below 4 in any category blocks an "approved" presentation; scores are review aids, not a substitute for the user's visual decision. Unresolved critical findings block promotion regardless of average score. The asset implementer cannot be the sole visual acceptor.

Proposed initial performance targets, to be qualified on named hardware rather than promised blindly: desktop standard profile aims at p95 frame time no worse than 16.7 ms; a declared mobile/low-power profile aims at 33.3 ms; input feedback within 100 ms; progress/cancellation remains responsive during long work. Measure startup and streamed-area transitions against explicit network conditions. If a target fails, optimise native residency, batching, LOD and work scheduling while preserving the accepted visual composition. Record revised device-specific budgets before expansion.

## 13. Swarm implementation order

The machine-readable [work packages](work-packages.json) define named roles, owned paths, dependencies and evidence. Run at most three specialist agents plus one integrating agent under the current concurrency limit. "Swarm" means coordinated independent work with contracts; it does not mean uncontrolled simultaneous edits to shared manifests or expensive builds.

### Wave A — establish the truthful baseline

Freeze the rejected preview as evidence. Reconcile current source routes, data, tests, benchmark workloads and all module/capability owners. Produce the complete migration ledger, public-data manifest rules, native instrument contracts and reference captures. Finish the art bible and scene storyboards. No new quality claims are made in this wave.

### Wave B — prove the entire production path at representative scales

Produce three substantial, reviewable native demonstrations: (1) the field studio/catchment with real mapped terrain, botanical evidence and restored visual density; (2) an Earth-to-space scientific instrument with real data and scale/coordinate handling; (3) an infosphere/commons-to-proof-lab journey with a real rights-sensitive query and a real test/benchmark. Each includes the underlying dataset browser, functioning controls, accessible equivalent and export. These are implementation proofs, not the final scope limit.

Show actual native interactive scenes and matched screenshots for review before mass-producing the remaining environments. A rendered concept may guide art direction, but cannot pass the native experience gate. Resolve material hydration, geometry/LOD, lighting, data contracts and instrument reliability here so defects are not multiplied across the world.

### Wave C — complete environments and data packs

Build all remaining realm assets, acquisition/normalisation packs, native instruments and module narratives. Process the coverage ledger by families with bounded handoffs. Add each completed instrument to the world and direct index simultaneously. Maintain a running count of unimplemented, blocked, implemented and independently verified records; do not substitute percentage-of-files for feature completion.

### Wave D — complete migration and repair

Finish every legacy feature disposition, all test and benchmark profiles, deep-link continuity, human-facing documentation and source bug fixes. Reconcile new capabilities added since the baseline. Native Lab UI and complete underlying data delivery are mandatory here; the temporarily preserved browser interface is not the destination.

### Wave E — qualify and publish

Perform whole-site artistic review, audience task tests, internationalisation/accessibility review, device/performance runs, error/recovery tests and complete coverage reconciliation. Produce the concrete release candidate and evidence bundle before requesting any final publication approval that is needed. Publish only the reviewed manifest and retain rollback. Do not make deployment or acceptance decisions from a successful build alone.

## 14. Coordination, cost discipline and handoffs

The integrator owns contracts, shared registries, publication graph, release decisions and the consolidated coverage ledger. Specialists own disjoint asset/data/instrument packages. A reviewer receives source identity, commands, output identities, actual images/results and known limitations; it does not simply repeat the implementer's self-assessment.

Before an expensive production batch, validate one representative asset and its complete material/data path. Reuse immutable outputs by content identity. Keep rendering, compilation and benchmark workloads bounded; do not run multiple full benchmark suites or full native builds to investigate a small unrelated change. Inspect existing function registries before re-auditing or reimplementing capability.

Each package starts with an estimate of asset count, source-data volume, render/build work, dependencies and uncertainty derived from the prototype—not invented calendar promises. Record actual effort and output reuse. Escalate a failed quality gate to a concrete diagnosis and correction; do not spend repeated cycles on exposure tweaks when the missing work is surface data or ecological staging.

Every handoff includes: owned paths/diff, source revision, input/output contracts, generated resources and receipts, tests actually run, inspected views, limitations, dependencies and next acceptance action. "File exists", "build passes" and "looks good" without evidence are rejected handoffs.

## 15. Definition of the completed overhaul

The work is complete only when the entire required coverage ledger is closed by evidence; all underlying permitted data and capability demonstrations are usable within the new environment; the core modules have human explanations and working roles; the worlds meet independent and user visual review; tests/benchmarks retain their meaning; and access, language, performance, recovery and publication gates pass.

The final delivery includes the website repository, native builds, authored source works and assets, data publications, complete instrument catalogue, real run records, benchmark archives, source/provenance inspectors, reviewed locales, documentation, redirects, reproducible release manifest and maintenance instructions. Remaining unsupported device or external-service conditions are explicit and testable. Incomplete work is not rebranded as a finished showcase.
