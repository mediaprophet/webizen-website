# Webizen website: the natural world and the infosphere

Design brief revised 16 November 2026 from the user's review. **The first three mock-ups are rejected.** They are retained only as design history. They do not establish the production renderer, visual standard, information architecture or approved composition.

## The subject of the website

Working positioning: **human-centric computing, grounded in the natural world.** This is a proposed umbrella, not an approved final brand line. It includes personal and shared devices, AI, scientific computation, creative expression, applications, communications, hosting, and the human-centric web/internet ecosystem.

Webizen is a human-centric computing ecosystem. Its website must explain what that means for people living in a physical, biological, social and cultural world. Physics, nature, the biosphere, biology, sociology and the liberal arts are primary subject matter. Their intersection with the infosphere is the organising principle.

An explorable world gives this subject spatial form. It should feel inhabited, ecologically connected and culturally meaningful. Technology supports activities within that world: observing, understanding, making, communicating, caring, deciding, cooperating and remembering. Module names become discoverable through those activities, with direct technical routes for visitors who already know what they need.

The reference to Myst concerns exploratory pace, environmental storytelling, spatial coherence, curiosity and cinematic atmosphere. Create original places, objects, visual language and narratives. The website is not a puzzle game that withholds information until a visitor solves a challenge.

## What failed in the first studies

- Three small platforms and generic monuments reduced an ecosystem to disconnected product metaphors.
- Flat material treatment, sparse geometry and simple lighting fell below the existing website's visual ambition.
- The scenes did not demonstrate the actual platform renderer or technical workflows.
- Popup explanations substituted for meaningful environmental interaction.
- Natural systems, human society and the liberal arts were mostly absent.
- The full technical-demo collection was not readily available.

Do not extend that renderer into the production site. Reuse only independently useful narrative research, source receipts and test findings. A real-time canvas alone is not evidence of artistic or technical excellence.

## One continuous world, multiple scales

Proposed starting world: an inhabited coastal catchment, from ocean and atmosphere through forest and settlement to organisms, observation instruments and information systems. A river connects upland geology, soil, biodiversity, human activity and the sea. Movement across scale connects the cellular and molecular world to landscape and planetary systems. This is a design proposal, not an approved final setting.

These are connected environments, not six isolated module islands. A tree can participate in ecology, geometry, biology, art, sensing and knowledge. A song can connect culture, acoustics, authorship, cooperation and computation. Relationships are many-to-many in the content graph.

## The environment must perform, not merely symbolise

Every major exhibit has a short human question, an observable action and a genuine software result. The scene provides context for the result. An instrument that displays invented telemetry is decorative and cannot stand in for a technical demonstration.

Examples to qualify and implement:

1. Select a plant. Inspect its authored geometry and biological relationships, then follow a source assertion into the knowledge graph.
2. Examine a field recording. Switch between supported visual or acoustic representations while preserving its source, units and timing. Any perceptual mapping is explicitly identified.
3. Change a bounded terrain parameter. Execute the supported operation, show the changed output and its actual assumptions, then return to the landscape.
4. Open a shared community work. Inspect its proposed purpose, terms and applicable authority. Run a synthetic, bounded policy example with an explained result.
5. Enter the workshop. Change a production recipe, run Synth through an authorised host, inspect the output, replay it and download its package. Pre-produced variants are labelled as such.
6. Enter a library or performance space. Explore typography, language, documents, music, sound and motion as working media capabilities with accessible equivalents.
7. Inspect the execution instrument. Run a real benchmark or device probe, retaining its input, correctness result, runtime, hardware and raw measurements. A cinematic animation is never a benchmark.

The full implementation must include all existing technical demos in an owned inventory. Each gets a decision: integrate, re-author, repair, consolidate, or preserve as a clearly dated archive. No silent deletion. Each core capability needs a real runnable example or an accurately described support limitation. Existing demo HTML is source material, not automatically a qualified current experience.

## Visual and interaction requirements

Use composed foreground, middle distance and horizon; believable scale; coherent geology and vegetation; materially distinct surfaces; purposeful light; atmospheric depth; water and environmental effects where supported; and deliberate film-style framing and titles. Avoid decorative shader noise, random glowing lines and abstract monuments as substitutes for a world.

Movement needs spatial continuity, legible landmarks, meaningful proximity, geometry-aware picking, touch/keyboard alternatives and clear exits. Guided camera sequences must be skippable and pausable. Free exploration is optional. Every place is also reachable from a map, search and conventional navigation, with a persistent link to its reading route. Do not require game navigation to find an API, benchmark, institutional brief or investment information.

Production assets must come through the actual Synth/Hypermedia/QualiaDB/Etalon paths selected for the release, with original `.vibe` recipes, retained-input identity, resources and receipts. Existing platform capabilities should be used before implementing a parallel renderer. A temporary external renderer must be explicitly justified and cannot be promoted as proof of Etalon execution.

Keep scientific models, artistic interpretation and measured observations distinct. The world can be poetic; quantitative claims must remain accountable. Human health, environmental and policy examples need appropriate domain review before making substantive claims.

## Data architecture

Extend the website content graph with `place`, `natural_system`, `human_activity`, `discipline`, `work`, `module`, `capability`, `demo`, `observation`, `evidence`, `relationship`, `scene_asset` and `audience_route`. Each exhibit connects a human question to a natural/social/cultural context, source capabilities, runtime profile and accessible explanation.

Store the human explanation separately from imported technical facts. Use stable IDs so the same record drives the world object, conventional page, map, search, technical reference and machine-readable representation. A renderer does not become the source of truth for the content.

## Review stages before the full overhaul

1. **Approve this subject and world structure.** The previous three studies are rejected; no visual approval is assumed.
2. **Review genuine art-direction boards and scene blockouts.** Show at least three materially different compositions of the same natural/human subject, so styling can be compared without changing the purpose. Include daylight, atmosphere, scale, material and typography decisions.
3. **Review a platform-produced vertical slice.** One coherent place, one ordinary visitor story, at least three real technical interactions, their evidence paths, and the corresponding reading route. Demonstrate the actual selected renderer, not just a list of capabilities.
4. **Compare against the existing site's best experiences.** Use side-by-side desktop and mobile captures, interaction recordings and observed device performance. The new slice must clearly advance visual richness, usefulness and capability presentation.
5. **Explicit user design approval.** Only then expand the approved direction across the entire site. Preserve the opportunity to reject or revise a direction without accumulating a full implementation around it.

## Repository decision

Use **`webizen-website`** for the public ecosystem website. It owns editorial content, world design, website recipes, content schemas, module/demo imports, navigation, accessible reading views, visual components, migration and publication. Component repositories retain implementation, normative specifications, benchmark workloads and release evidence.

The local repository is `C:\github\webizen-website`. Creation of a remote, choice of remote organisation, publication and domain cutover are separate actions. No current website files are moved or removed as part of setting up this project.

## Cross-cutting requirements: human support and observer paths

The ecosystem's information, socioeconomic and knowledge geometry must be meaningful across all these places. Human rights principles, purposes, needs and support requirements are demonstrated through positive assistance, practical agency, boundaries, explanations and recourse—not merely described.

The `.q42`/Vibe-backed experience uses the existing status-of-observer and circumstance concepts to offer different paths through the same world. Presentation changes subtly with purpose, context and stated support requirements, while sources, evidence, rights and user control remain coherent.
