# Human rights, purposes, needs and support across the ecosystem

Controlling requirement added 16 November 2026. **Human-centric computing ecosystem** is the working umbrella. The website must demonstrate human rights principles, human purposes, needs and support requirements through observable behaviour. This is central to the site and the ecosystem, not a standalone policy page or a permission-dialog theme.

## Information, socioeconomic and knowledge geometry

Use this framing to connect the natural world, human life and the infosphere. The explorable experience should reveal relationships among people, purposes, needs, knowledge, resources, work, institutions, technologies and places. Those relationships affect what people can understand, create, access, contest and accomplish.

The website should let a visitor change a viewpoint, inspect a relationship, trace a consequence and discover the supporting evidence. A knowledge relationship, an information flow and a socioeconomic dependency are not interchangeable links. Each needs a type, context, source, perspective and meaningful explanation.

For example, a river observation connects physical conditions, ecological relationships, a person's knowledge, an institution's responsibilities, the resources needed to investigate, and the purposes for which information may be used. A creative work connects expression, authorship, learning, collaboration, access, resources and appropriate terms of use. These intersections belong within the world experience.

Keep mathematical claims precise: a drawn graph alone does not establish an implemented information-geometric metric, socioeconomic model or scientific result. Where a specific algorithm, metric, manifold, optimisation or inference is demonstrated, identify the implemented operation, inputs, units, assumptions and qualification. Do not reduce human rights or human worth to a score.

## What every major experience must answer

1. Whose purpose is being served, and who defined it?
2. What has the person stated they need or require support with?
3. Which rights principles and responsibilities are relevant, and how are they interpreted here?
4. What practical assistance does the ecosystem provide?
5. What choices remain with the person, and what can others do on their behalf?
6. Which information, resources, institutions and technologies make the activity possible?
7. What changes when a purpose, permission, need, fact or relationship changes?
8. How can someone understand, correct, challenge, withdraw from or seek human assistance with the outcome?

Demonstrate positive support as well as boundaries. A refusal can be appropriate, but a system that only says “no” does not demonstrate support for human needs. Provide useful permitted alternatives where the actual capabilities support them.

## Required demonstrations for the first approved vertical slice

The slice must include at least one positive support interaction, one meaningful purpose/authority boundary, and one correction or contestability interaction. Each must execute through the actual selected implementation and show its result and limits. Expand the following examples across the complete world.

| Experience | Person's purpose and support requirement | Observable behaviour | Actual implementation/evidence required |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Accessible participation in a living-world investigation | Understand an environmental finding using a person's chosen presentation and input modes | Switch among supported text, visual, captioned/audio and keyboard routes while preserving the question, source and result; save a preference only by explicit choice | Real UI and media paths; equivalent task completion; focus and interaction checks; captions/transcripts and source/result identity |
| Community environmental knowledge | Contribute an observation for a stated community research purpose, with appropriate access and attribution | Inspect who receives what; run the supported permitted request; attempt an out-of-scope request; show the actual outcome and explanation | Executable authored purpose/authority rules, retained request and result, actual host enforcement where claimed; no browser-only policy badge presented as enforcement |
| A delegated AI helper | Receive assistance with a bounded task while retaining agency and access to help | Inspect scope; authorise a supported operation; observe a refused or escalated out-of-scope operation; see the resulting work and an available human-controlled next step | Actual host capability and policy path, identified principal/delegate, qualified cancellation/revocation semantics and a record of the attempted action |
| A correction or contested interpretation | Correct an inaccurate assertion or challenge an interpretation without losing relevant context | Inspect source and uncertainty; submit a synthetic correction or dispute; rerun the dependent query or explanation; see what changed and what remains contested | Versioned graph/assertion path, actual query/inference output, source attribution and traceable difference; supported conflict handling rather than silent overwrite |
| Creative expression and supported collaboration | Make and share a work using tools suited to a person's capabilities, available resources and chosen terms | Produce an accessible variation with Synth; inspect editable origins; examine actual sharing scope and authorship information; reopen the package | Real `.vibe` execution, media resources, HMC/replay receipt, accessible output inspection and supported publication/host behaviour |
| Cooperation, work and socioeconomic participation | Understand responsibilities, resource needs and options in a shared activity | Change a stated support/resource requirement; inspect who or what is affected; compare qualified options and show unresolved tradeoffs; retain meaningful human choice | Explicit actors, resources, dependencies and rules; real cooperation/work-graph or scheduling operations where used; no automatic claim of fairness or rights compliance from an optimisation result |

These are proposed scenarios for qualification. Do not claim that all end-to-end workflows already work because their underlying functions exist. Conversely, do not describe existing implementations as missing merely because the website has not integrated them.

## From principle to executed behaviour

Keep the chain inspectable:

**Human principle or instrument → explicitly attributed interpretation → authored requirement → actual policy/support mechanism → observed behaviour → evidence and limits → recourse.**

Normative references should identify their source, jurisdiction/scope when relevant, interpretation owner and revision. Project interpretations must not be presented as official institutional positions. A tool receipt or passing test does not establish universal human-rights or legal compliance.

Show the person's goal first. Let readers open the deeper chain progressively. Government, UN/civil-society, developers, researchers, ordinary visitors and investors need different depth, but the facts and evidence must remain consistent across those views.

## Data contract

Every promoted exhibit should link these records where applicable:

- Human activity, declared purpose, principal and affected participants.
- Stated needs and support requirements, with source and consent/access scope; no inferred sensitive profile presented as fact.
- Rights-principle references, attributed interpretation and applicable responsibility.
- Information/knowledge relationships, resources, institutional and socioeconomic dependencies.
- Actual capability, execution host, runtime profile and supporting module owners.
- Authored support/policy rules, proposed action and executed outcome.
- Explanation, evidence, uncertainty and available alternatives.
- Correction, contestation, withdrawal/revocation or human-support route, with actual limitations.
- Accessible interaction and reading equivalents.

Use synthetic people and records in public demonstrations. Real-world cases need appropriate participation, provenance and publication authority. Do not ask visitors to disclose sensitive personal needs just to explore the site.

## Acceptance

An evaluator must be able to observe a meaningful change in behaviour when the relevant purpose, requirement, permission or information changes. A label changing while the underlying action remains unaffected fails. A hardcoded “allowed” response presented as executed policy fails. A generic accessibility statement without task equivalence fails. A recourse button without a functioning or honestly described route fails.

Positive support, practical agency, comprehensible relationships, accountability and technical truthfulness are as important as visual quality. The art direction should make these relationships easier to understand and feel, while the actual platform demonstrates what it can do.
